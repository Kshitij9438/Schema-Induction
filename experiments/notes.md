# Experiment Notes — Schema Induction Project

## Purpose

This document records experimental observations, architectural decisions,
failures, and research conclusions throughout the development of the
Schema Induction system.

The goal is not to justify outcomes, but to understand the strengths,
limitations, and boundaries of the proposed architecture.

---

# Experiment Timeline

## Phase 1 — Initial Prototype

### Objective

Determine whether weak supervision can induce latent semantic roles
without explicit labels.

### Initial Pipeline

Corpus

↓

Pair Generation

↓

Frozen DistilBERT

↓

Projection Head

↓

Contrastive Training

↓

Role Clustering

↓

Extraction

The initial prototype successfully learned meaningful latent structure,
but suffered from implementation issues that were later discovered.

---

# Phase 2 — Token Alignment Investigation

## Problem

Early experiments produced unstable clusters,
particularly for person and temporal tokens.

Investigation revealed that token positions were being produced by one
tokenization pipeline while embeddings were produced by another.

The pipeline effectively became

tokenizer.tokenize()

↓

encoder(...)

which introduced silent token↔embedding misalignment due to special
tokens (CLS/SEP), subword splitting, and tokenizer-specific behavior.

---

## Resolution

A single canonical interface was introduced:

```python
TokenEncoder.encode_with_tokens()
```

Every component now consumes aligned

```
(tokens, embeddings)
```

pairs generated by the encoder itself.

This removed duplicate tokenization logic from

- training
- embedding dump
- clustering
- visualization

and established the encoder as the single source of truth.

---

# Baseline Capability

## Amount Role

### Observation

Amount tokens form the strongest and most stable latent role.

Examples

- 100
- 250
- 450
- 1200
- 5000

remain tightly clustered across experiments.

### Conclusion

Numeric entities behave as atomic semantic roles and are learned
consistently through weak supervision.

---

## Item Role

### Observation

Items form a coherent semantic region.

Examples

- coffee
- books
- groceries
- shoes
- snacks

The cluster is weaker than the numeric role but remains interpretable.

### Conclusion

Token-level role induction is sufficient for many concrete entity roles.

---

## Function Words

Function words consistently form their own region.

Examples

- for
- with
- the
- and
- was

This indicates that the projection head separates grammatical glue from
semantic content.

---

## Temporal Role

### Observation

Temporal tokens appear within coherent regions but do not consistently
form a dedicated role.

Examples

- today
- yesterday
- morning
- night

remain less stable than amount or item roles.

---

### Interpretation

Time behaves differently from atomic entities.

Unlike amounts or objects,
temporal information usually modifies events rather than existing as an
independent semantic role.

This is therefore considered an architectural limitation rather than an
implementation failure.

---

# Surface-Form Robustness

The corpus was intentionally diversified using

- inverted word order
- telegraphic phrases
- omitted verbs
- person-leading sentences
- time-leading sentences

without changing the model architecture.

### Observation

Role geometry remained stable.

Numeric and item clusters persisted.

No catastrophic collapse occurred.

### Conclusion

The model is learning structural regularities rather than memorizing
sentence templates.

---

# Quantitative Evaluation

Current baseline metrics

| Metric | Value |
|----------|-------:|
| Silhouette Score | 0.2832 |
| Davies–Bouldin Index | 1.4789 |
| Calinski–Harabasz Score | 47.59 |

### Interpretation

The learned role space exhibits measurable latent structure.

Clusters remain partially overlapping,
indicating significant room for improvement through better supervision
rather than architectural complexity.

---

# Architectural Conclusions

The current formulation assumes

```text
role = f(token_embedding)
```

This assumption works well for

- values
- entities
- grammatical tokens

It is less effective for

- temporal modifiers
- relational concepts
- multi-token spans

These represent the current representational boundary of Version 1.

---

# Lessons Learned

The largest improvements did **not** come from increasing model
complexity.

Instead, the most significant gains came from

1. fixing token/embedding alignment,
2. establishing a single tokenizer interface,
3. improving experimental reproducibility.

This suggests that implementation correctness and supervision quality
have a larger effect on representation quality than additional neural
network depth.

---

# Current Bottleneck

The limiting factor is no longer

- the encoder,
- the projection head,
- the loss function,
- or the optimizer.

The limiting factor is now the quality of the weak supervision signals
generated in

```
data/pairs/build_pairs.py
```

Future improvements should therefore focus on signal specification rather
than architectural expansion.
